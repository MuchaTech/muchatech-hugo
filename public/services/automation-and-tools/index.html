Manual work is a security problem

Every hour your team spends copying alerts into a spreadsheet is an hour not spent investigating one. Toil spreads because each workaround is small and individually reasonable — until the process lives in one person’s head, at the end of a long week.

The same patterns show up in most automation assessments:

  • Alerts are triaged by hand, in the order they arrive rather than the order that matters.
  • Compliance evidence is assembled from screenshots when an auditor asks.
  • Onboarding and offboarding depend on someone remembering every step.
  • A production or operational metric is re-typed into a report every Monday.
  • Two systems hold the same data and nobody trusts either copy.

Automation is not about replacing people. It is about making the process repeatable, observable, and safe to change.

What we do not do

We do not sell licences, run six-month discovery projects, or hand you a black box. Every engagement produces a written design before code, a working increment before handover, and a mechanism for telling you when something breaks.

Where a small tool is the answer, we publish it: see the tools catalog for the calculators and checks we maintain.

  1. 01

    Discover Week 1

Questions clients ask

What does a project cost?

Builds start at R15 000 for a single, well-scoped automation. Multi-workflow programmes are quoted after the design step, and a support retainer covers monitoring and small changes.

How long does it take?

Two to four weeks for a single workflow, four to ten weeks for a multi-system build. The design step gives a firm date before the build starts.

Do we need to buy a platform?

Not necessarily. We work with Power Automate, n8n, your existing SOAR platform or plain code, and only recommend a licence where the build genuinely needs one.

Who owns the code?

You do. Source, configuration and documentation are handed over at the end of the project, so there is no lock-in and no licence to keep paying.

Can you automate a process we already run manually?

Yes — that is the usual starting point. We map the current process first, because automating a broken process only makes the failure faster.

What about industrial and OT environments?

We have built tooling for industrial workshops and production data. Work is scoped around uptime: read-only collection first, dashboards second, and any change inside a maintenance window.

What happens when an automation fails?

Every build includes failure handling and alerting. Silent failure is the one outcome we design against.
