Most security training is forgotten by Monday

An annual slide deck and a quiz does not change what someone does when an invoice arrives with changed banking details. People fall for the same handful of attacks because the training was something they watched, not something they practised.

The formats on this page are built around practice: a scenario that runs, a decision that gets made, a habit that gets tested, and a report that shows whether anything changed.

What practised teams do differently

  • They recognise the specific lures used against their sector, not security theory.
  • They know who to call first — and it is not the helpdesk queue.
  • Their runbook has been read aloud under pressure, by the people who would use it.
  • Leadership has already made the difficult call in a drill, so the real incident is not the first time.
  • Click rates and reporting rates are measured, so training budget goes where it changes behaviour.

Training is not a substitute for the controls on our managed cybersecurity service — it is what makes those controls work when a person, not a tool, is the target.

Questions clients ask

What does a session cost?

Sessions start at R7 500 for a half-day workshop of up to 20 people. Drills, phishing campaigns and multi-day technical sessions are quoted after scoping, and travel outside Gauteng is quoted separately.

Do you deliver on-site or online?

Both. On-site sessions work best for drills and leadership exercises; online sessions work well for awareness and technical clinics, provided attendees can keep cameras on and the group stays under 20.

How many people can attend?

Twenty per session is the practical maximum for a workshop that stays interactive. Larger teams are covered with repeat sessions, or with a leadership briefing plus a train-the-trainer session.

Is the phishing simulation safe?

Yes. We agree the campaign in writing first, never capture credentials, never use real threats or public shaming, and always debrief the people who click. The goal is a measurable habit change, not embarrassment.

Do participants get a certificate?

Participants receive an attendance record you can keep for audit purposes. We do not issue accredited certifications and will not describe the training as accredited.

Can you train staff who are not technical?

That is the core audience for the awareness workshop. Sessions use plain language and examples from your own environment, with no jargon and no assumed security background.

How do you measure whether it worked?

Depending on the format: a pre- and post-session quiz, a phishing baseline and retest, drill observations against your runbook, and a follow-up review with the coordinator.
